A variant hyper-focused on rushing out massive Fusion monsters right out of the gate.

Arguably one of his most frustrating decks because it specifically targets and shuts down your magic cards.

• Key Cards: Imperial Order (negates all Spells), Royal Decree (negates all Traps), Nobleman of Crossout, and Vorse Raider.

A psychological variant that relies heavily on RNG and chance to pick apart your field.

• Key Cards: Multiple copies of Barrel Dragon (coin flips to destroy monsters) paired up with Fairy Box to make attacking him incredibly risky.

5. The Hand & Board Control Variant (Max Stars)

A brutal iteration designed to strip you of your resources before you can even take a turn.

1. The High-ATK Aggression Rule (Normal Summon Priority)

Kaiba’s AI does not pass turns passively if it can avoid it.
• The Logic: Scan the hand for the Level 4 monster with the highest absolute ATK value (La Jinn, 7 Colored Fish, or Battle Ox).
• The Execution: Immediately Normal Summon it in face-up Attack Position, regardless of what face-down cards the opponent has. Kaiba is programmed to confidently assume his monster will overpower yours. He only Sets a monster face-down if his entire hand consists of cards with under 1500 ATK (like Saggi or Versago).

2. The One-for-One "Spot Removal" Sequence

If you manage to put a monster on the field that is stronger than his Level 4 beatsticks, Kaiba doesn't panic—he instantly initiates a standard spot-removal algorithm to clear the path.
• The Logic: If Opponent's Monster ATK > Kaiba's Highest Level 4 ATK, activate removal.
• The Execution: He will play Tribute to the Doomed or Ring of Destruction.
• The Hard-Coded Follow-up: The absolute micro-second your monster is destroyed by his Spell/Trap, Kaiba will instantly summon his highest ATK Level 4 monster and push for a direct attack. He will never use a removal spell and then just pass the turn.

3. The Steal-and-Smash (The Power Spells)

Kaiba runs a heavy lineup of iconic theft cards (Change of Heart, Snatch Steal, and Soul Exchange). When he draws these casually, he treats them as pure math adjusters.
• The Logic: If he has a high-level monster in his hand (Blue-Eyes White Dragon or Summoned Skull), he checks if your monster can be used as food.
• The Execution: He will activate Change of Heart or Snatch Steal to take your monster, and then immediately Tribute it to Normal Summon his Summoned Skull or Blue-Eyes White Dragon.
• If he has no Tribute monster: He will still activate Change of Heart, take your strongest monster, and attack you with your own card alongside his own monsters to maximize life point damage that turn.

4. The Blind Aggressive Spell Sequence

The AI in Kaiba the Revenge does not hoard card advantage. If it gets a generic powerhouse spell, it uses it immediately to force momentum.
• Pot of Greed / Graceful Charity: Played the absolute instant Main Phase 1 begins.
• Mystical Space Typhoon / Heavy Storm: If you have even one card in your Spell/Trap zone, Kaiba will blindly activate MST or Heavy Storm before he does anything else, just to guarantee his upcoming summon won't hit a Trap Hole or Mirror Force.

5. The "Giant Germ" Wall Loop

If Kaiba's hand is terrible and he is losing the match, his AI flips to a defensive stalling sequence.
• The Logic: Prevent direct damage at all costs while keeping tribute targets ready.
• The Execution: He will Set Giant Germ face-down. When you destroy it in battle, the AI forces the chain block: you take 500 damage, and Kaiba instantly pulls the remaining two Giant Germs from his deck in Face-up Defense Position. This buys him two more turns of blocking while building a perfect field to tribute for Blue-Eyes White Dragon on his next turn.

🌊 Torrential Tribute (The Over-Aggressive Board Wipe)

The AI handles this card with zero patience. It does not wait for a massive field setup; it values board reset above card advantage.
• The Trigger Condition: Activated the exact microsecond any monster is Summoned (Normal, Flip, or Special) by either player, provided the opponent controls at least one face-up monster.
• The AI Quirk: Kaiba will often accidentally destroy his own established field. If he flips his own Cyber Jar, the AI’s loop registers the incoming special summons and will immediately chain Torrential Tribute—wiping out the very monsters he just drew and summoned.

💥 Ring of Destruction (The Maximum Damage Calculator)

The AI views this card strictly as an emergency health-bar execution or a tool for massive chip damage.
• The Trigger Condition: Activated during the opponent's Main Phase or the moment an attack is declared in the Battle Phase.
• Target Selection Priority: The AI scans the field and selects the face-up monster with the absolute highest ATK. It does not care if the monster belongs to you or Kaiba himself.
• The Self-Destruct Flaw: The 2004 AI engine fails to calculate lethal damage to itself. If both you and Kaiba are at 1500 Life Points, and you summon a monster with 2000 ATK, Kaiba will confidently activate Ring of Destruction, destroying the monster and reducing both of your Life Points to 0—resulting in a sudden, accidental Draw/Tie.

⚰️ Call of the Haunted (The Immediate Battle Counter)

Kaiba rarely uses this card during his own turn to build combos. Instead, it is hard-coded as a defensive reflex or an end-of-turn resource grab.
• The Trigger Condition:
	1. Defensive Trigger: Activated exclusively when you declare a direct attack on his Life Points. He will immediately chain Call of the Haunted to revive his highest-ATK monster from the Graveyard, forcing a replay or an unfavorable battle trade for you.
	2. End-Phase Trigger: If you pass your turn without attacking, the AI will activate it during your End Phase to freely pull a monster back right before his turn begins.

🥊 Fairy Box (The Mandatory Upkeep Rule)

This is his primary psychological stall tool, and its execution rules are entirely automated.
• The Trigger Condition: Flipped face-up the very first time you declare an attack against any monster on his side of the field.
• The Maintenance Logic: During Kaiba's Standby Phase, the AI runs a simple math check: If Kaiba's LP > 500, pay the cost. He will continuously bleed his own Life Points dry to keep this card active, only allowing it to self-destruct if paying the 500 LP upkeep would completely drop his health to 0.
